## Why it matters
The death cap (Amanita phalloides) causes most fatal mushroom poisonings. It is common under oaks in coastal California, especially after fall and winter rains.

## Lookalikes
Young death caps can resemble edible mushrooms, including paddy straw mushrooms familiar from Asian cooking and young puffballs. Cooking does not destroy the toxin.

## Features
Death caps typically have a greenish to yellowish or white cap, white gills, a ring on the stem, and a cup-like sac at the base, which may be buried in soil. Features vary with age and weather, so they cannot be used to prove a mushroom is safe.

## Symptoms
Symptoms usually begin 6 to 24 hours after eating: vomiting, diarrhea, and cramps. People often seem to recover, then suffer liver failure days later. Anyone who may have eaten one needs emergency care immediately, even without symptoms.
